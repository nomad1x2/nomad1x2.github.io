Task 1 - Getting Started - (Forensics)

You arrive on site and immediately get to work. The DAFIN-SOC team quickly briefs you on the situation. They have noticed numerous anomalous behaviors, such as; tools randomly failing tests and anti-virus flagging on seemingly clean workstations. They have narrowed in on one machine they would like NSA to thoroughly evaluate.

They have provided a zipped EXT2 image from this development machine. Help DAFIN-SOC perform a forensic analysis on this - looking for any suspicious artifacts.

Downloads:

  • zipped EXT2 image (image.ext2.zip)

    • Prompt:

  • Provide the SHA-1 hash of the suspicious artifact.

    • Solving the task:

    This was a very straight forward task to open up the 2025 NSA Codebreaker Challenge. We're given a Linux file system image and are told to look for any suspicious artifacts, and right away I mount it and look at the root history:

    Out of all the requests, one stood out as suspicious by downloading a file called "get.sh" and piping it straight into sh: 

    nomad@LAPTOP-7SLS1EV9:~$ grep -Rin sh /mnt/task1/root -C 2

    The solution:


    This seemingly creates a payload called "c", and copies it into the runlevels/sysinit directory as "mnarvexcws". This means that whatever was downloaded/created is able to run at boot -- this must be the smoking gun. Sure enough, I identified the file, grabbed the sha1sum (35f03767455f94e0ad70527a827ab8206597fd9c), and uploaded into the solver:

    Great job finding that artifact! Let's report what we found to DAFIN-SOC leadership.
